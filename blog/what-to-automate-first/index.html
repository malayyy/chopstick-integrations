What should you automate first?

A practical framework for choosing automation by frequency, risk and business impact.

CHOPSTICK INTEGRATIONS · 6 min read · 7 NOVEMBER 2026

Automation is most valuable when it removes repeatable effort without hiding important decisions. Start with the work that happens often, follows clear rules and creates measurable friction.

Score the workflow

Assess frequency, time spent, error cost and rule clarity. A task performed dozens of times a day with predictable inputs is a stronger candidate than a rare process requiring judgment.

  • Frequency: how often does it happen?
  • Time: how much effort does each instance consume?
  • Risk: what happens when a step is missed?
  • Clarity: can the team explain the rules and exceptions?

Good first candidates

Lead assignment, duplicate checks, appointment reminders, document routing, status notifications, data synchronization and recurring report preparation are often strong candidates.

Keep human judgment in the loop

Pricing exceptions, sensitive customer conversations, legal decisions and unusual complaints should not be handed to an unattended workflow just because they repeat. Automate preparation and routing; keep accountable people responsible for consequential decisions.

Automate the handoff first. Automate judgment only when the decision boundary is safe, explicit and testable.

Build observability from day one

Every workflow needs a success path, failure path, logs, retry rules and an owner. Track execution failures and business outcomes, not only the number of runs. Pilot one process, measure the baseline and expand only when the evidence supports it.
